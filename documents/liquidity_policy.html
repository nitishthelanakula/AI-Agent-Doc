Liquidity Coverage Policy

The Treasury team must maintain sufficient high-quality liquid assets to cover expected net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario.

Primary sources of liquidity include cash at central banks, overnight deposits, short-dated government securities, and committed borrowing lines approved by the Treasury Committee.

Liquidity buffers should be monitored daily and reported to management if the projected coverage ratio falls below 1.10x.

Any shortfall against policy limits should be escalated to the Head of Treasury and the ALCO chair before the end of the reporting day.

Excess Cash Placement Limits

Excess cash may be placed only with counterparties and instruments approved under the liquidity policy.

Single-counterparty concentration limits apply to deposits, money market funds, and repos. The maximum exposure to a single non-bank counterparty is 10% of total liquidity resources unless specifically approved.

For banks, deposits must be within approved credit limits and maturity buckets. Long-dated placements are restricted when liquidity coverage is under stress.

Daily Treasury Reporting

Daily cash, funding, and liquidity reports should reconcile opening balance, inflows, outflows, and net liquidity position to the previous close.

Material variances greater than 5% versus forecast must be explained and reviewed by treasury operations.
