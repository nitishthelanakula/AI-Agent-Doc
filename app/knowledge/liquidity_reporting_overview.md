# Liquidity Reporting Overview

## Purpose

The treasury liquidity reporting process provides a consistent view of cash, funding, and risk exposure across the reporting period. The objective is to support management decisions with timely, accurate, and explainable information.

## Core reporting concepts

- Cash positions: current and projected balances by legal entity, currency, and account.
- Inflows and outflows: expected movements from operations, financing, and treasury actions.
- Liquidity buffer: available cash and highly liquid assets that can absorb short-term stress.
- Policy thresholds: internal limits, covenant conditions, and minimum liquidity coverage requirements.

## Reporting cadence

Reporting should be produced at a defined frequency, with the latest operational values validated against source systems before publication. Any difference between source data and treasury output should be reviewed before the report is shared with stakeholders.

## Review expectation

The reporting package should clearly identify which values are sourced directly from systems, which values are calculated, and which values require manual judgement. Where assumptions are used, the support logic should be documented and re-checked as part of the review cycle.
